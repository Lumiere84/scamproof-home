One rule covers almost every scam

Scammers no longer need to fool your eyes and ears. So we stop trusting what we see and hear, and start trusting how we check.

STOP.

Pressure is the scam. Pause. Don't pay, don't click, don't share.

CALL.

Hang up. Contact the person or company yourself, on a number you already trust.

CHECK.

Tell a family member before any money or information moves.

The four levers every scam pulls

Scams change every year. The levers underneath them don't. Learn these four and you can spot scams you have never seen before.

Lever 1

Emotion

Fear, love, excitement or shame switch off careful thinking.

Your grandson is in jail.
Lever 2

Urgency

Real problems can wait ten minutes while you check. Scams can't.

Right now, or it's too late.
Lever 3

Secrecy

A second person would spot the trick, so scammers keep you alone.

Don't tell anyone.
Lever 4

Strange payment

Gift cards, wires, crypto, payment apps or a cash courier. Very hard to get back.

A courier will pick it up.

One word stops the voice-clone call

The "family emergency" call uses a voice that sounds exactly like your child or grandchild. A family safe word beats it every time.

  1. Pick a word or short phrase that has never been online. Not a pet, street or birthday.
  2. Share it in person or by phone. Never by text, email or social media.
  3. Agree: any emergency call asking for money must include the word.
  4. No word? Hang up and call your loved one on the number you already have.

What to say on the call

"I want to help. What's our family word?"

If they can't answer:

"I'm going to hang up and call you back."

Then do it. You are never foolish for hanging up to check.

Ask for our family word. No word = no money.

No gift cards. No codes. No "safe accounts."

If a scam already got through

Act fast — speed matters more than embarrassment. You did nothing shameful. Criminals did.

Do these in order

  1. Call your bank or card company on the number on the back of your card. Say: "I've been scammed. Please stop the payment and secure my account."
  2. Paid with gift cards? Call the number on the back of the card right away and ask them to freeze the funds. Keep the card and receipt.
  3. Change the password of any account you shared, starting with your email.
  4. Gave someone remote access to a computer? Disconnect it from the internet and have it checked.
  5. Shared your Social Security number? Freeze your credit at all three bureaus and visit IdentityTheft.gov.
  6. Report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and online fraud to the FBI at ic3.gov.
  7. Tell your family.

Watch for the second scam. "Recovery experts" who promise to get your money back for a fee are scammers too.
